Liyue's character names feel unusually rich because they are written inside an existing language and cultural tradition. A name may sound graceful in English while also evoking a medicinal herb, a classical expression, a natural image, or a deliberate pun in Chinese.

This guide looks at the Chinese characters behind well-known Liyue names, how they are pronounced in Mandarin, and what associations a Chinese reader may hear. It is an independent linguistic reading, not a claim about the developers' private naming process.

## What Does Liyue Mean?

**Liyue** is written **璃月** (*Líyuè*).

- **璃** appears in words connected with colored glaze, crystal, or glass, such as 琉璃 (*liúlí*).
- **月** means "moon."

The combined name can therefore suggest something like **glazed moon**, **crystal moon**, or **moon of luminous glass**. It does not sound like the name of an ordinary modern Chinese city. It sounds elevated and constructed, which suits a fantasy region shaped by mountains, minerals, contracts, commerce, and divine history.

The important lesson is that Chinese names are often understood through both **sound and written characters**. Pinyin alone cannot show the full meaning.

## Zhongli: 钟离

**Zhongli** is written **钟离** (*Zhōnglí*).

钟 can mean "bell" or "clock," while 离 has meanings including "to leave," "to separate," and the trigram associated with Fire. Reading the two characters separately is tempting, but **钟离 is also a traditional compound Chinese surname**. Compound surnames contain two characters, like 欧阳 (*Ōuyáng*) and 司马 (*Sīmǎ*).

That makes Zhongli sound old, formal, and historically grounded. It is a good example of why a Chinese name should not always be translated character by character. Sometimes the unit has a naming history beyond its literal parts.

## Xiao: 魈

**Xiao** is written **魈** (*Xiāo*), using a rare character associated with a mountain spirit or demon in traditional vocabulary.

This is not the everyday character **萧**, which appears in ordinary surnames and names, nor **晓**, meaning dawn. The unusual graph immediately gives the name a supernatural and archaic atmosphere to a Chinese reader.

As a real-life given name, 魈 would be highly unconventional. It works for a fantasy character precisely because it carries an uncanny association. This distinction matters when adapting game-inspired names: a beautiful fictional name is not automatically a natural modern personal name.

## Ningguang: 凝光

**Ningguang** is written **凝光** (*Níngguāng*).

- **凝** means to concentrate, congeal, or become still.
- **光** means light, radiance, or brilliance.

Together, the name can be read as **concentrated light**, **gathered radiance**, or **light held still**. The image feels polished and controlled rather than impulsive. It fits the language of refinement, wealth, architecture, and calculated power.

凝光 resembles a poetic expression more than a common contemporary given name. It is understandable and elegant, but intentionally literary.

## Keqing: 刻晴

**Keqing** is written **刻晴** (*Kèqíng*).

- **刻** can mean to carve, engrave, or mark a moment.
- **晴** means clear weather or a clear sky.

The combination is uncommon as an ordinary name. A reader may hear an image like **carving clarity** or **a precisely marked clear sky**. The hard, decisive feeling of 刻 contrasts with the bright openness of 晴.

This is a useful naming technique in fiction: pair a character suggesting action or discipline with one suggesting an ideal state.

## Ganyu: 甘雨

**Ganyu** is written **甘雨** (*Gānyǔ*).

- **甘** means sweet, pleasant, or willingly accepted.
- **雨** means rain.

More importantly, 甘雨 is an established literary expression for **welcome or timely rain**, especially rain that nourishes crops after drought. The phrase can symbolize grace, relief, and benefit arriving when needed.

Unlike a random combination of "sweet" and "rain," 甘雨 already carries cultural resonance as a complete expression. It demonstrates how strong Chinese names often borrow a compact image with a history of use.

## Xingqiu: 行秋

**Xingqiu** is written **行秋** (*Xíngqiū*).

- **行** has several pronunciations and meanings. Here it is read *xíng*, associated with going, traveling, acting, or moving through.
- **秋** means autumn.

The name can evoke **traveling through autumn** or **autumn in motion**. It has the cadence of a poetic phrase and suits imagery of books, wandering, swordsmanship, and cultivated leisure.

The multiple readings of 行 also show why tone marks matter. Without characters or context, a learner may not know whether to read it *xíng* or *háng*.

## Chongyun: 重云

**Chongyun** is written **重云** (*Chóngyún*).

- **重**, pronounced *chóng* here, means repeated, layered, or again.
- **云** means cloud.

The resulting image is **layered clouds** or **cloud upon cloud**. Chinese landscape poetry frequently builds atmosphere through clouds, peaks, mist, moonlight, and changing weather. 重云 therefore feels like a landscape condensed into two characters.

## Xiangling: 香菱

**Xiangling** is written **香菱** (*Xiānglíng*).

- **香** means fragrant or aromatic.
- **菱** is the water caltrop, an aquatic plant with an edible fruit.

The name combines scent and a specific plant image. 菱 has long appeared in feminine names and literary descriptions. It sounds softer and more name-like than directly using the name of a prepared medicine.

## Hu Tao: 胡桃

**Hu Tao** is written **胡桃** (*Hú Táo*).

Read as a personal name, **胡** is a common surname and **桃** means peach. But written together, **胡桃** is also a word for **walnut**.

This creates immediate wordplay for Chinese readers. The spaced English form preserves the surname-given-name structure, while the unspaced Chinese word produces the joke. It is one of the clearest examples of a Liyue name operating on two levels at once.

## Baizhu: 白术

**Baizhu** is written **白术** (*Báizhú*).

白术 is the Chinese materia medica name for **Atractylodes macrocephala**, an herb used in traditional Chinese medicine. The second character is pronounced *zhú* in the herb name, even though 术 is commonly read *shù* in words meaning art, method, or technique.

The reference is especially fitting for a character associated with a pharmacy. Yet 白术 is much more recognizable as an herbal term than as an ordinary modern given name. Someone seeking a botanical Chinese name could take inspiration from its method without necessarily adopting it unchanged.

## Qiqi and Yaoyao: Nickname Patterns

**Qiqi** is **七七** (*Qīqī*), literally "seven seven." **Yaoyao** is **瑶瑶** (*Yáoyáo*), repeating 瑶, a character associated with precious jade.

Repeating a syllable is common in Chinese nicknames and affectionate childhood names. It can make a name sound intimate, youthful, or endearing. The pattern does not mean every doubled character works equally well; the base character and social context still matter.

## Yun Jin, Shenhe, Yelan, and Yanfei

Several other names build compact cultural images:

- **云堇, Yún Jǐn (Yun Jin):** 云 is cloud; 堇 can refer to a violet-like plant. The space in English reflects a surname and given name.
- **申鹤, Shēn Hè (Shenhe):** 申 is a surname; 鹤 is crane, a bird associated with longevity and transcendence.
- **夜兰, Yèlán (Yelan):** 夜 means night; 兰 means orchid. "Night orchid" is elegant and mysterious, though more literary than typical.
- **烟绯, Yānfēi (Yanfei):** 烟 is smoke or mist; 绯 is scarlet. The pairing creates a vivid color image.

## What Liyue Names Teach Us About Chinese Naming

Liyue names use several real techniques found in Chinese naming and literary language:

1. **Compact natural imagery:** clouds, rain, light, orchids, cranes, and autumn.
2. **Classical vocabulary:** uncommon characters can create historical or supernatural atmosphere.
3. **Established expressions:** 甘雨 carries more meaning as a phrase than its two separate glosses suggest.
4. **Botanical references:** plants can imply fragrance, resilience, season, or cultivated character.
5. **Wordplay:** 胡桃 functions as both a personal name structure and an ordinary noun.
6. **Sound-character interaction:** the same pinyin syllable may correspond to many different characters and meanings.

## Can You Use a Liyue Name in Real Life?

You can use a character name as a screen name or fandom nickname, but copying it as a personal Chinese name deserves more thought.

Ask three questions:

- Does it sound like a modern human name or intentionally like a fantasy title?
- Does the character have obscure, supernatural, medicinal, or comic associations?
- Will native speakers recognize it primarily as a famous fictional character?

Names such as 魈 and 白术 carry very specific meanings. Others, such as 晴, 云, 兰, 瑶, and 鹤, can provide inspiration when combined carefully with different characters.

### Why are some Liyue names written with a space in English?

Names such as Hu Tao and Yun Jin represent a family name followed by a given name. Other localized forms are written as one word. English spacing is useful, but the Chinese characters provide the clearest structure.

### What does Ganyu mean in Chinese?

甘雨 literally combines "sweet" and "rain," but it is also a traditional expression for welcome or timely rain that nourishes the land.

### Is Baizhu a Chinese herb?

Yes. 白术 (*báizhú*) is the traditional materia medica name of Atractylodes macrocephala. Its pronunciation is specific to the herbal term.
